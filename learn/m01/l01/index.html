What Is an LLM?

Distinguish language-model capabilities from verified knowledge and reasoning.

By the end of this lesson, you will understand what a Large Language Model (LLM) is, how it produces answers, what it can do well, and why a convincing answer is not always a correct answer.

You will practise using AI for everyday tasks, recognising unreliable information, making better decisions, and explaining AI confidently to someone who has never used it.

Imagine This Real-World Situation

You have recently started working as a customer support assistant at an international company.

One morning, your manager asks you to prepare an email for a customer whose order has arrived late.

You open an AI assistant and type:

Your request:

Write a polite email apologising to a customer for a late delivery.

Within seconds, the AI produces:

Illustrative AI response:

Dear Customer,

We sincerely apologise for the delay in delivering your order. We understand how frustrating this must be. Our team is working to resolve the issue, and we appreciate your patience.

Kind regards, Customer Support Team

The email sounds professional. It is clearly written, polite and useful.

Your manager is impressed.

Then you ask the AI:

Has the customer's package already left our warehouse?

The assistant replies:

Yes. Your package left our warehouse yesterday and will arrive tomorrow.

Before reading on: can this assistant confirm the package status?

There is a serious problem.

The AI has not been given the order information. It has not checked the warehouse system.

How could it know where the package is?

It cannot reliably know that from the information provided.

This is the central challenge of working with language models:

An AI assistant can produce a very convincing answer without having evidence that the answer is true.

The email is a useful piece of writing. The delivery claim needs verification.

By the end of this lesson, you will know how to recognise this difference and respond correctly.

02

What Exactly Is an LLM?

LLM stands for Large Language Model.

Let's understand the three words.

WordSimple meaning
LargeThe model is typically trained using very large amounts of data and contains many learned numerical values.
LanguageIt works with language-related information, such as words, sentences, instructions and computer code.
ModelIt is a mathematical system trained to recognise patterns and produce predictions.

A Large Language Model is an AI system trained to process and generate language by learning patterns from substantial amounts of data.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the broader field of building computer systems that can perform tasks associated with human intelligence.

An LLM is one type of AI, not every type of AI.

Examples of products that use language models include ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. Different products may use different models and additional tools.

Why Is It Called a Model?

Imagine learning English by reading conversations, stories, articles and examples.

Over time, you notice patterns:

Questions often begin with words such as what, where or why.

A polite business email usually begins with a greeting.

A recipe usually contains ingredients and instructions.

An explanation should connect ideas in a logical order.

A language model learns statistical patterns from training data.

Training is the process of adjusting a model's internal numerical values so it becomes better at performing its intended tasks.

It does not simply keep an ordinary notebook of every sentence it has read. It learns numerical relationships that help it interpret and generate text.

An important difference is that the model learns through mathematical optimisation, not through human life experience.

How Does an LLM Generate an Answer?

A common foundation of modern LLMs is predicting the next token.

A token is a small unit of text. It may be a whole word, part of a word, a punctuation mark or another text fragment.

Consider this sentence:

Please send the invoice by ...

Possible continuations include:

email

Friday

tomorrow

Which continuation makes sense depends on the surrounding words.

The surrounding information is called context.

Modern LLMs use a neural network, a mathematical system of connected computational units, to calculate possible continuations from the context.

They generate a response step by step, usually producing one token after another.

A simplified view of text generation

StageWhat happens
1. Your promptYou enter: "Explain photosynthesis to a child."
2. The model processes the contextIt calculates likely and useful next tokens using patterns learned during training.
3. The model generates a responseFor example: "Plants use sunlight to help make their food..."

Real models are more complicated than this simplified process.

Their generation process can support explanations, planning, calculations and sophisticated problem-solving.

Predicting tokens does not mean a model can only finish simple sentences. It can learn patterns that support surprisingly complex abilities.

However, generating a plausible answer is not the same as checking that every claim is true.

A Useful Analogy: An Extremely Advanced Writing Assistant

Imagine a writing assistant that has studied an enormous variety of examples.

You ask it to create a meeting invitation.

It recognises what a meeting invitation usually contains and creates a suitable message.

But suppose you ask:

Did Maria accept tomorrow's meeting?

If the assistant cannot access your calendar or an invitation response, it does not have evidence of Maria's decision.

Its ability to write a realistic meeting message does not give it access to actual meeting attendance.

Where the analogy stops: An LLM does more than copy writing patterns. It can work with complex relationships, analyse provided information and perform reasoning tasks.

The analogy only helps explain why fluent writing should not be confused with verified facts.

What Can an LLM Do Well?

LLMs can be useful in many situations.

CapabilityEveryday exampleWhat you should remember
WritingDrafting a professional emailReview names, facts and promises
SummarisingMaking a long report easier to readCheck that important meaning remains
ExplainingExplaining a difficult idea in simple EnglishVerify important technical claims
TranslatingTranslating a customer messageCheck sensitive or specialised wording
BrainstormingSuggesting names for a new projectTreat suggestions as ideas, not facts
CodingHelping write or explain computer codeTest the code before relying on it
ReasoningComparing options against given criteriaCheck the steps, assumptions and conclusions

Worked Example: Turning Difficult English Into Simple English

Original text:

We regret to inform you that your application cannot be processed due to insufficient supporting documentation.

Your request:

Rewrite this sentence in simple, friendly English without changing its meaning.

Illustrative AI response:

We are sorry, but we cannot process your application because some required documents are missing.

This is a good use of an LLM because the task is to rewrite information already provided.

The assistant does not need to invent facts about the application.

Worked Example: An Incorrect Assumption

Your request:

A customer says their refund has not arrived. Explain why the payment is delayed.

Illustrative AI response:

Your bank is experiencing a temporary processing problem.

This answer might sound reasonable, but the request contains no information about the bank.

A better response is:

I cannot determine the reason for the delay from the information provided. Please check the refund status and payment records before explaining the cause.

Notice the difference.

The first answer invents a possible cause and presents it as fact.

The second answer identifies the missing information.

That is a much safer approach.

04

Three Different Things: Generation, Reasoning and Verified Knowledge

These three ideas are related, but they are not identical.

Generating Language

Language generation means creating text based on the prompt, surrounding context and patterns learned during training.

Example:

Write a friendly birthday message for my colleague.

The main goal is suitable wording.

Different messages could all be acceptable.

Reasoning

Reasoning means using information to work through a problem, connect ideas or reach a conclusion.

An LLM can perform reasoning tasks, sometimes very effectively. However, it can also make mistakes in logic, arithmetic or assumptions.

Consider:

A company has 12 laptops. It buys 5 more and gives away 3. How many remain?

A correct solution is:

12 + 5 − 3 = 14.

The calculation follows the supplied facts.

Now compare:

A company has 12 laptops. How many laptops will it have next Friday?

We do not know.

The company might buy, sell, lose or replace equipment.

A reliable assistant should explain that the future number cannot be determined without more information.

Good reasoning includes recognising when the available information is insufficient.

Verified Knowledge

Verified knowledge, in this lesson, means a factual claim supported by reliable evidence that has actually been checked.

For example, suppose an assistant says:

The conference begins at 9:00 a.m.

How can you know whether that is correct?

You might examine the official conference schedule.

If the schedule confirms that the event starts at 9:00 a.m., you have evidence supporting the claim.

But if the assistant simply writes that the event begins at 9:00 a.m., its statement is not itself proof.

A source may also be outdated, incorrect or unrelated. Good verification involves checking whether the evidence genuinely supports the specific claim.

SituationWhat it demonstratesIs the claim verified?
AI writes a polite greetingLanguage generationNo factual verification is normally needed
AI correctly solves a supplied arithmetic problemReasoning and calculationThe result can be checked mathematically
AI guesses a delivery datePlausible text without sufficient evidenceNo
AI reports a date that matches an official current schedule you checkedA factual answer supported by evidenceYes, within the scope of that evidence
AI provides a source link that you have not openedA potentially checkable referenceNot yet

The main lesson is not that LLMs cannot reason or cannot provide accurate knowledge.

They can.

The lesson is that capability, correctness and verification are different questions.

Case 3 — Arriving at a Meeting

The meeting starts at nine, so please arrive ...

  1. earlier
  2. purple
  3. sandwich

Answer: A — earlier

Earlier is a suitable continuation because the speaker wants the listener to arrive before the meeting starts.

Correct. It is grammatically and contextually suitable.

Incorrect. Purple is usually a colour and does not appropriately describe when someone should arrive.

What Did You Discover?

The same word may be suitable in one sentence and unsuitable in another.

Context influences what a model generates.

However, even a suitable continuation can contain a factual claim that has not been checked.

For example:

The meeting starts at nine.

The continuation may be grammatically correct, but that does not prove the real meeting starts at nine.

Key learning: Generating language that fits the context is different from confirming real-world facts.

06

Why Can an LLM Sound Correct When It Is Wrong?

Imagine an AI assistant producing this answer:

The company was founded in 2014 by Daniel Morris.

The sentence is grammatically correct.

It sounds natural.

It provides a specific year and a specific person.

But none of those qualities proves that the statement is true.

The model might have produced incorrect information, confused two companies or filled an information gap with an unsupported detail.

This type of error is often called an AI hallucination.

An AI hallucination occurs when a model generates inaccurate, fabricated or unsupported information and presents it as an answer.

Examples include invented citations, nonexistent books, incorrect historical dates and imaginary product features.

Why Does This Happen?

Language models learn patterns that help them produce plausible and helpful text.

The training process does not guarantee that every generated statement will be factually correct.

Errors may also come from ambiguous questions, missing context, imperfect training information or failures in multi-step reasoning.

Even advanced models can make these mistakes.

Does a Confident Answer Mean the Model Is Certain?

No.

Consider two responses.

Response A:

The policy definitely allows all customers to receive a full refund after 90 days.

Response B:

I cannot confirm the refund period without checking the company's current policy.

Response A sounds more confident.

But unless a relevant policy has been checked, that confidence is not evidence.

Response B is more responsible because it recognises the information gap.

In real work, it is often better to say "I don't have enough information to confirm that" than to create a false impression of certainty.

Can Asking the Same Question Again Verify an Answer?

Not by itself.

Suppose an AI says a historical event happened in 1986.

You ask the same question again, and it repeats 1986.

That may make the answer seem more convincing.

However, both answers may come from similar learned patterns. Agreement between model outputs is not independent evidence.

To verify the date, you need an appropriate source such as a reliable historical archive or authoritative reference.

08

How to Use an LLM Responsibly

A practical approach has four steps.

Step 1 — Identify Your Goal

Are you asking the AI to write, explain, suggest, calculate or confirm a real-world fact?

Knowing the task helps you decide how much verification is necessary.

Step 2 — Provide Relevant Context

Include the necessary information, constraints and desired format.

Do not enter private customer records or sensitive details unless your organisation has explicitly approved that use.

Step 3 — Examine the Answer

Look for unsupported names, dates, statistics, promises, calculations and assumptions.

Ask what the AI actually had access to.

Step 4 — Verify Before Acting

Check consequential facts using reliable evidence.

Review calculations, test code and confirm operational claims using appropriate authorised records.

An Improved Request

Instead of asking:

Explain why the customer's order has not arrived.

Try:

Draft a polite response to a customer whose order is late. We have not confirmed the reason for the delay. Do not invent a cause or delivery date. Explain that we will check the order status and provide an update.

This improved prompt clearly tells the model what is known and what must not be assumed.

A good prompt reduces some risks, but it does not guarantee that every output will be correct.

Human review and appropriate verification are still important.

Common Misunderstandings About LLMs

Before moving to independent practice, review these common mistakes.

MisunderstandingMore accurate understanding
"An LLM is a giant search engine."A language model generates responses. Some AI products also connect models to search systems and other tools.
"If an answer sounds professional, it must be correct."Writing quality does not establish factual accuracy.
"An LLM cannot reason."LLMs can perform reasoning tasks, but their reasoning can be incorrect.
"An LLM always understands a situation exactly like a human."A model can process context and make sophisticated inferences without having human experiences or direct awareness of events.
"Every AI answer needs an internet search."Creative writing and rewriting supplied text often do not require external factual verification.
"Giving a source link proves the answer."The source must be genuine, relevant and actually support the claim.
"If the AI repeats an answer, it has confirmed it."Repetition is not independent verification.
"AI is useless because it sometimes makes mistakes."AI can be extremely helpful when its capabilities, limitations and appropriate checks are understood.

The Model and the Complete AI Product

A language model and an AI assistant application are not necessarily the same thing.

The application may provide additional abilities, such as searching the web, reading authorised documents, using a calculator or interacting with approved software.

These external functions are called tools.

An LLM can request or help coordinate tool use when the application supports it.

The surrounding software performs the tool operation and makes its results available to the model.

For example:

Without access to a company's records, the model cannot confirm the latest order status.

With an authorised, working order-management tool, an assistant may be able to retrieve a status record.

Even then, the returned information must be interpreted correctly, and the answer should not claim more than the record establishes.

The presence of a tool does not automatically guarantee a correct answer.

A Practical Checklist You Can Use at Work

When an AI assistant produces an answer, ask yourself:

0 of 6 checks completed.

How to use the checklist: Review each statement and tick it when you have completed the relevant check.

If any important check remains incomplete, investigate before relying on the output.

To reuse the checklist for another AI-generated answer, clear all ticks and review the new answer from the beginning.

Completing the checklist does not replace evidence checks where they are required.

14

Lesson Recap

You have explored how language models generate useful responses and why they must be used with informed judgement.

Remember these essential ideas:

An LLM is a trained AI model that processes and generates language using learned patterns.

Tokens and context matter. Modern LLMs generate text step by step, using surrounding information to guide their predictions.

LLMs have real capabilities. They can write, explain, summarise, translate, code and perform reasoning tasks.

Fluency is not proof. A professional-sounding answer may contain an unsupported claim.

Reasoning can be strong but fallible. Check important calculations, assumptions and conclusions.

Verification requires evidence. An answer becomes more trustworthy when appropriate reliable sources or records actually support it.

Responsible users know when to check. A creative draft and a financial claim do not require exactly the same review process.

  2. Context forces the model to use exactly the same words every time.
  3. Context removes the need to review the generated output.
  4. Context gives the model surrounding information that helps it determine suitable responses.

Answer: D — Context gives the model surrounding information that helps it determine suitable responses.

The model uses available context to interpret the request and generate relevant continuations. More relevant context can improve the answer, but it cannot guarantee correctness.

Context can contain incorrect or incomplete information.

Models can produce different answers even with the same context.

Context does not eliminate factual or reasoning errors.

Correct. The model uses available context to interpret the request and generate relevant continuations. More relevant context can improve the answer, but it cannot guarantee correctness.

5. Which task is primarily an example of language generation rather than factual verification?

  1. Confirming whether a real customer received a refund.
  2. Writing a fictional birthday greeting in a friendly tone.
  3. Checking the current price of a specific airline ticket.
  4. Determining whether a real parcel has left a warehouse.

Answer: B — Writing a fictional birthday greeting in a friendly tone.

A fictional greeting mainly requires suitable language. The other tasks involve real-world facts that need appropriate evidence.

Refund status should be checked against an authorised payment or refund record.

Correct. A fictional greeting mainly requires suitable language. The other tasks involve real-world facts that need appropriate evidence.

Current ticket pricing requires reliable and sufficiently recent information.

Real shipment status must be established from appropriate shipping information.

6. An AI assistant solves a supplied arithmetic problem correctly. What does this demonstrate most directly?

  1. That the model has verified every fact in the world.
  2. That all future answers from the model will be correct.
  3. That the model has successfully performed the calculation in this particular case.
  4. That the model must have accessed an external database.

Answer: C — That the model has successfully performed the calculation in this particular case.

A correct solution provides evidence of success on that problem. It does not prove universal accuracy or show which internal or external methods were used.

Solving one problem does not verify unrelated factual information.

Correct performance on one task cannot guarantee future performance.

Correct. A correct solution provides evidence of success on that problem. It does not prove universal accuracy or show which internal or external methods were used.

A model may perform arithmetic without retrieving information from an external database.

The shipping claim requires evidence from an appropriate source. Without that evidence, it remains unverified.

Correct. The shipping claim requires evidence from an appropriate source. Without that evidence, it remains unverified.

Confidence does not establish delivery status.

Repeating a claim does not independently verify it.

Polite wording cannot correct an unsupported factual statement.

8. What is an AI hallucination?

  1. A model producing several different but acceptable creative stories.
  2. A model translating a sentence into another language.
  3. A model asking for clarification when necessary information is missing.
  4. A model generating inaccurate, fabricated or unsupported information as part of its answer.

Answer: D — A model generating inaccurate, fabricated or unsupported information as part of its answer.

Hallucinations include invented references, incorrect dates and unsupported factual claims. A response may sound completely natural while containing these errors.

Variation in creative writing is not necessarily a factual error.

Translation is an ordinary language-model capability.

Requesting clarification can help avoid unsupported assumptions.

Correct. Hallucinations include invented references, incorrect dates and unsupported factual claims. A response may sound completely natural while containing these errors.

9. Which statement about LLM reasoning is most accurate?

  4. A model can reason correctly only when every answer has already been stored word for word.

Answer: B — Language models can perform reasoning tasks, but their reasoning and conclusions may be incorrect.

Modern LLMs can solve complex problems, compare alternatives and develop multi-step explanations. However, they can make logical mistakes or rely on unsupported assumptions.

LLMs demonstrate useful reasoning abilities across many tasks.

Correct. Modern LLMs can solve complex problems, compare alternatives and develop multi-step explanations. However, they can make logical mistakes or rely on unsupported assumptions.

Generated reasoning must not automatically be assumed correct.

Models can solve new problems without retrieving an identical stored answer.

10. An AI assistant provides a website link as evidence for a factual claim. What should you do before treating the claim as verified?

A link alone does not prove anything about the claim.

Professional design is not a reliable test of factual accuracy.

Correct. Reliable verification requires evaluating the evidence itself. A citation may be incorrect, outdated, fabricated or unrelated to the statement.

AI answers can contain valid, useful references. They still require appropriate review.

11. You ask an AI for the reason a customer cancelled a subscription. The cancellation record contains no reason. Which response is most responsible?

There is no evidence that cost caused the cancellation.

Correct. This response accurately separates the known cancellation status from the unknown cause. It does not invent a reason.

14. A company wants an AI to draft a customer message about a delayed refund. Which instruction best reduces unsupported claims?

15. Which statement best describes responsible use of an LLM at work?

The AI has helped us prepare a clear message, which is useful.
